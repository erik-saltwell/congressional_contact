# Project context

## Overview

Congressional Contact is a web page that lets a person quickly look up their two U.S. senators and their U.S. House representative and save those officials' contact details to their phone.

**Problem.** It is hard for people to reach their representatives when they want to influence a vote. Nearly every senator and representative publishes phone numbers, plus web forms or email addresses. But finding them in the moment, during a busy day, is a barrier to making contact.

**Solution.** A page where a user can look up their officials and download their details as contacts, as quickly and easily as possible.

**Why it works.** Once the contacts are on the phone, leaving a quick message at an office when the user wants a vote to go a certain way takes far less effort.

## Design goals

**Primary goal.** The page succeeds if every visitor who is on a phone, knows what the site is, and wants their officials' contact cards leaves with those contacts on their phone. That is the whole measure of success.

The page should be as clear, simple, and easy to use as possible.

**Secondary goal (half as important as the primary).** People who want to copy the page and self-host it can do so as easily as possible.

## Settled decisions

- **Officials per visit:** three: the user's two senators and their one House representative.
- **Primary user (P0):** someone who can recognize the name of their own House representative. Supporting people who cannot is a lower priority (P1).
- **Card contents:** full detail wherever the data has it: phone numbers, web form, email, and office addresses.
- **Delivery:** a single click that loads all three contacts at once, if that is possible without compromising the other goals. If it is not, fall back to one download per official.
- **Success condition:** the contacts are actually saved in the phone's contacts app. A completed file download is acceptable if the platform cannot do better.
- **Self-hosting:** static files copied to any web host, with no backend, accounts, or API keys. Ideally the whole page is one file.

## Open questions

These were deliberately not decided yet:

- How the user is matched to their officials (for example, picking a name or state versus entering an address or ZIP code).
- Where the official and contact data comes from, and how a static, no-API-key page keeps it current.
- Whether a single-click multi-contact import works reliably on both iOS and Android.
